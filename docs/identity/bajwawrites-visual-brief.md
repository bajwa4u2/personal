# Bajwa Writes — Visual Brief

**Status:** Pre-asset reasoning. Implements Section 2 of
`AURA_ECOSYSTEM_IDENTITY_SYSTEM.md` and Amendment 1 (wordmark
sufficiency). This document does not produce assets. It decides
what, if anything, deserves to be produced.

**Approval gate:** Asset generation for the Bajwa Writes identity
does not begin until this brief is approved by the founder, in
writing.

---

## Headline conclusion

**Wordmark-only.** Bajwa Writes does not require a symbol at the
identity level. The wordmark, set in Source Serif 4 italic, carries
the brand at every touchpoint the brand needs to reach. A small
**colophon device** may eventually exist — but it lives at the
page-level scale of a printer's mark on the imprint page of a bound
edition, *never* as a brand glyph on web chrome, favicons, or
masthead surfaces.

**Recommendation:** **symbol prohibited at the brand level; symbol
optional at the printer's-device level (imprint page only).**

The reasoning follows.

---

## 1. Does Bajwa Writes require a symbol?

**No.**

The doctrine in Section 2.5 of the parent document already concluded
that the mark is *almost no mark*. This brief examines whether any
of the still-permitted primitives (codex, page proportion, gathering,
colophon, reading column, serif letterform) cross from *permitted*
to *required* — and finds that none do.

Three tests against the wordmark-sufficiency principle (Amendment 1):

1. **Does the wordmark fail at any touchpoint Bajwa Writes needs to
   reach?** No. The wordmark renders at favicon scale (16 px,
   28 px Retina), masthead scale, OG-card scale, and book-cover
   imprint scale. The serif italic survives every size.
2. **Is there something a symbol could express that the wordmark
   cannot?** Almost — the *physical bound edition* and the
   *centuries-old tradition of printer's devices* are not encoded
   in the wordmark alone. But those expressions belong on books and
   imprint pages, not on the identity surface. They are page-level
   ornament, not brand mark.
3. **Would a symbol compete for primacy?** Yes, immediately. Any
   glyph beside a serif wordmark in italic shifts the reading from
   "publication house" to "publication-house-with-icon" — the
   register slides toward startup or content-platform aesthetics,
   both rejected in Section 2.2.

The publishing-house precedent reinforces the verdict. Faber &
Faber's identity is the wordmark. Knopf carries a borzoi as a
printer's device, not as a corporate logo. Penguin's penguin lives
on book spines, not on Penguin's brand chrome — the corporate
identity is the wordmark. The lineage of literary publishing is
wordmark-first.

## 2. Can Bajwa Writes function entirely as typography?

**Yes — at every touchpoint that matters for brand identity.**

The wordmark, set in Source Serif 4 italic, semibold weight, in
sentence case ("Bajwa Writes" — never `Bajwa Writes™`, never
`BAJWA WRITES`), is the full visual identity. The typeface is
already canonised in the ecosystem (Section 5.4). The italic carries
the *literary* register that distinguishes Bajwa Writes from Aura
and Orchestrate inside the shared family. The serif carries the
preservation register.

A test: imagine a reader encountering the wordmark in five
contexts — a browser tab favicon, a website nav, a book spine, an
OG card on social, an imprint page in a PDF. In each context the
wordmark reads as a literary publisher. No symbol is needed to
clarify the category. The typography does the work.

## 3. If a symbol exists, what does it represent?

The only symbol that earns the right to exist for Bajwa Writes is
the **colophon device** — and it exists at the page level, not at
the brand level. The distinction is doctrinal:

- A **brand logo** announces the publisher at every surface where
  the publisher's voice is heard.
- A **colophon device** is a printer's mark that closes a bound
  edition. It is the publisher's signature *inside the book*, set
  in restraint at the foot of the imprint page, after the
  copyright notice and before the back cover.

If (and only if) a colophon device is later commissioned, it
represents:

- **The bound edition itself** — the sealed artifact, distinct
  from a clip-art book icon (Section 2.4 categorically excludes
  the latter). A colophon device might be a small abstract glyph
  reading as a gathering of pages, a sewn signature, a sealed
  envelope, or a typeset ornament in the tradition of historical
  printers' marks.
- **The continuity of authored work across editions.** The same
  device appears on every bound edition the publisher issues,
  signalling that the editions belong to one shelf.

The problem it solves: when a reader opens a Bajwa Writes book in
fifty years, the colophon page should identify the publisher
without requiring the wordmark to repeat itself larger than the
work. A small device is more dignified than a repeated wordmark.

If no colophon device is ever commissioned, no harm is done. The
wordmark on the title page already names the publisher.

## 4. Smallest acceptable symbol

**14 × 14 px** monochrome, only ever as a printer's-device
ornament on the imprint page of a bound edition or PDF. Below
that size it becomes illegible and should not render at all (use
the wordmark instead).

A symbol at this scale is a *visual full stop*: it occupies the
slot a period-after-the-final-line would occupy. It does not
participate in branding.

## 5. Largest acceptable symbol

**96 × 96 px** equivalent, only ever at the foot of the imprint
page of a bound edition, where the colophon traditionally sits.
Never larger. Never on a cover. Never on a masthead. Never as a
home-page hero.

The largest the colophon device may appear in any digital context
is at the foot of a publication-page colophon block, alongside the
canonical URN.

## 6. Browser tab strategy

**Favicon: typographic, not a glyph.**

The favicon at `bajwawrites.com` should be:

- **Primary candidate.** A single Source Serif 4 italic lowercase
  `b` in ink-on-cream (or paper-on-ink in dark register), rendered
  at favicon resolutions (16, 32, maskable variants). This is the
  publication's printed initial — the kind of initial that
  historically opened a chapter on parchment. It is not a
  monogram; it is a typographic full-character.

- **Acceptable alternative.** A tiny rectangular gathering
  ornament — three or four short horizontal strokes stacked,
  reading as a stacked signature of pages — if it survives the
  16 px legibility test. Untested in this brief; would require its
  own brief if pursued.

- **Rejected.** A bold `BW` monogram (the withdrawn drift), a
  book emoji, a quill, any clip-art device.

Both candidates honour the wordmark-only doctrine because neither
is a *symbol* in the brand-mark sense — the first is a single
typographic character, the second is a paragraph-ornament shape.
Neither participates in the marketing voice.

The current production favicon uses the default Flutter logo (per
the ecosystem audit). Replacing it with the typographic `b` is a
defensible asset task *after* this brief is approved. Until then
the Flutter default is the lesser-but-temporary placeholder.

## 7. Website masthead strategy

**Wordmark alone.**

At `bajwawrites.com` and on every internal surface, the masthead
is the wordmark "Bajwa Writes" set in Source Serif 4 italic at the
appropriate display weight for the surface's typographic scale
(matching the institutional canon's hero / page-header type
ladder).

No icon to the left. No tagline to the right. No ornament beneath
the wordmark.

The page itself — the reading column at literary line length, in
warm-paper register, with Source Serif 4 body — is the brand
context. Adding an icon would visually compress the literary
register into a content-platform register.

The current Flutter app's `PageShell` already follows this rule.
This brief codifies it.

## 8. Book-cover strategy

**The cover belongs to the work, not to the publisher.**

The cover composition of any Bajwa Writes title is:

1. **Title** — the work's title in display typography chosen for
   that specific edition.
2. **Author** — the author's name in subordinate typography.
3. **Cover art (optional)** — chosen per-work, as a deliberate
   editorial decision; not a brand-template overlay.

The publisher's identity appears on the cover **only as the
wordmark "Bajwa Writes" at the foot of the back cover, or on the
spine**. Never as a stamp on the front face. The front face is
the work's; the spine and the back are the publisher's.

The colophon device, if it exists, may appear on the imprint
page *inside* the book — never on the cover.

The three current covers (Burden / Edge / Origin) already follow
this rule and need no rework under this brief.

## 9. Publication-page strategy

**Wordmark in the nav. Reading column in the centre. URN at the
foot. No logo on the chrome.**

The current `PublicationDetailSurface` (built in the prior
session) already implements this. Specifically:

- The nav header carries "Bajwa Writes" as a quiet trail label
  ("Bajwa Writes · Archives · The Burden of Knowing"), in sentence
  case.
- The hero pairs the cover image with the title block; both belong
  to the *work*, not to the brand.
- The synopsis, themes, and author note sit in the reading column.
- The colophon at the foot carries the canonical URN
  (`bw:publication/<slug>`) — and is the *only* place the
  printer's-device colophon glyph would ever appear.

This brief approves the existing surface as canonical and
prohibits the addition of any logo glyph elsewhere on the page
chrome.

## 10. Social-preview strategy

**Typographic OG card. The work's cover does the image work.**

For each publication, the OG card is:

- **Background:** dark institutional register
  (`#0E1326`/`#151B33`), matching the ecosystem.
- **Primary visual:** the publication's own cover image, full-
  bleed on one side or contained at 2:3 proportion.
- **Type block:** title of the work (display serif) + author
  ("Muhammad Sakhawat") + publisher wordmark ("Bajwa Writes") at
  the foot in subordinate weight.

For the Bajwa Writes home/index, the OG card is purely
typographic:

- **Background:** dark register.
- **Primary type:** the wordmark "Bajwa Writes" in Source Serif
  4 italic at the optical centre.
- **Subordinate type:** "A literary publication house" or
  similar one-line clause in sentence case.
- **No glyph overlay.**

The current `bajwawrites/web/index.html` has no OG image (per the
audit). Generating one is a permissible asset task *after* this
brief is approved.

## 11. PDF / literary document strategy

A Bajwa Writes PDF is structured as a book interior — not as a
marketing document:

1. **Title page** — work title (large display), author (smaller,
   below), publisher wordmark "Bajwa Writes" at the foot in
   restraint, all in Source Serif 4.
2. **Copyright page** — standard copyright notice, ISBN if any,
   "Published by Bajwa Writes" naming the imprint in body
   weight, content-addressed URN in JetBrains Mono, revision
   hash, sealed date.
3. **Body** — reading column at literary line length, single
   column, generous leading, Source Serif 4 body.
4. **Imprint / colophon page** (last page, before the back
   cover) — full colophon text in restraint: "Set in Source Serif
   4. Published by Bajwa Writes, an imprint of Aura Platform LLC.
   Sealed to revision `<hash>` on `<date>`."

The colophon device, if it exists, appears here — at the foot of
the imprint page, ≤ 96 × 96 — and nowhere else.

## 12. Prohibited / rejected / permanently excluded

The following directions are categorically excluded by Section 2.4
of the parent doctrine and are reaffirmed here without exception:

### Prohibited (no exception, no amendment without redoing the doctrine)

- **Clip-art book icons** — square book emoji, open-book glyphs,
  closed-book glyphs. Reads as e-reader app.
- **Pen nibs, fountain pens, quills, inkwells, parchment scrolls
  with quills.** Reads as note-taking app, signature service,
  fantasy RPG, or freelance-writer platform.
- **A "BW" monogram** in any treatment — bold, ligated, stencilled,
  scripted. This is the withdrawn drift the parent doctrine
  superseded; it does not return.
- **Open book with stars, sparkles, motion lines, glow.** Reads as
  "learning platform".
- **Brush-stroke abstract signatures**, "creative" loops, "personal-
  brand" gestures. Reads as consultancy.
- **The author's photograph as the publisher's mark.** A face is
  not a publishing house's identity.
- **Any social-media-derived iconography** — heart, share, comment,
  bookmark, save, "for you" arrow.

### Rejected (declined in this brief; could be reconsidered in a
future explicit amendment, with reasoning)

- **An ornate gothic / blackletter wordmark.** Decorative
  blackletter reads as either a metal band, a college emblem, or a
  beer brand — none of which is the register. The current Source
  Serif 4 italic carries enough literary weight; blackletter would
  push past restraint.
- **A two-line wordmark stacked** ("Bajwa" over "Writes"). The
  single-line treatment is the canonical form.
- **A wordmark with an underline, overline, or rule frame.** Adds
  no semantic weight; reads as decorative bordering.
- **Any colour other than the warm paper / deep ink register on
  Bajwa Writes surfaces** (or the dark institutional register on
  shared surfaces, per Section 5.5). No accent on the wordmark.

### Permanently excluded motifs

These appeared in earlier explorations and are nailed shut:

- The `BW` monogram in any form.
- The triangular bookmark glyph.
- Open-book-with-quill composites.
- Any animated favicon or animated masthead element.
- Any branding device that overlaps with Aura's typed-gate
  iconography or Orchestrate's circular-arrows iconography. The
  three product identities must be distinguishable on a single
  screen with no captions.

---

## Recommendation

| Decision | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **Brand-level symbol** | **Prohibited.** Wordmark only. |
| **Page-level colophon device** | **Optional.** May be commissioned later for use at the foot of imprint pages and PDF colophons only. ≤ 96 × 96 cap. Never on web chrome, never on covers, never on mastheads. |
| **Favicon** | A single Source Serif 4 italic `b` recommended. Treated as typography, not as a symbol. Subject to its own pre-asset sub-brief if commissioned. |
| **OG card** | Typographic for the brand index; cover-led for individual publications. |

The default state is wordmark-only. A future task may legitimately
commission a colophon device for bound editions; that task will
need its own short brief and explicit approval. Until then no
glyph is generated under the Bajwa Writes identity.
