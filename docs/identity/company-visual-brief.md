# Aura Platform LLC (Company) — Visual Brief

**Status:** Pre-asset reasoning. Implements Section 4 of
`AURA_ECOSYSTEM_IDENTITY_SYSTEM.md` and Amendment 1 (wordmark
sufficiency). This document does not produce assets. It decides
what, if anything, deserves to be produced.

**Approval gate:** Asset generation for the company identity
does not begin until this brief is approved by the founder, in
writing.

---

## Headline conclusion

**Wordmark-only at the brand level. One narrow exception: a
silent, non-brand favicon device may exist at technical-square
slots (favicon, future operating-system app launcher entry) to
fill a slot the platform requires, *not* to brand.**

The company's voice in the ecosystem is its absence-of-voice —
the substrate beneath the three products, the institutional band
under every page, the quiet wordmark "Aura Platform LLC". A
competitive logo would inverte Section 4.2: *"not louder than
Aura. Not louder than Orchestrate."*

**Recommendation:** **symbol optional — narrow, technical, silent.**

The reasoning follows.

---

## 1. Does Aura Platform LLC require a symbol?

**Optional. The wordmark alone is preferred at every brand-voice
surface; a small functional device is permissible only where the
platform technically requires a square mark.**

The wordmark-sufficiency test (Amendment 1):

1. **Does the wordmark fail at any touchpoint the company needs
   to reach?** Almost no. Every long-form surface (site,
   investor deck, PDF, institutional band) renders the wordmark
   cleanly. The single failure mode is the *technical square
   slot* — a 16-px favicon at `company.auraplatform.org`, or the
   `<apple-touch-icon>` slot at iOS home-screen scale. At those
   slots, a wordmark cannot be set legibly (the type collapses);
   the browser draws a blank tile if nothing is provided.
2. **Is there something only a symbol could express at those
   slots?** Yes — *the company exists*. A blank tile reads as
   "site is broken / unconfigured / dead". A small device reads
   as "this is a deliberately-built thing". The expression is
   functional presence, not brand voice.
3. **Would a symbol compete with products on shared surfaces?**
   If brand-voiced: yes, immediately, and the doctrine
   prohibits this. If silent and abstract (a fragment of the
   already-canonised substrate-fingerprint diagram): no.
4. **Would the silent device survive restraint tests?** Yes if
   abstract, monochrome, drawn from an existing canonical
   diagram, and never used outside the technical-square slot.

The verdict: a *brand symbol* is rejected. A *favicon device*
that is technically functional and visually silent is
permitted under tight constraints, documented below.

## 2. Can the company function entirely as a wordmark?

**Yes — at every surface that carries brand voice.**

The wordmark composition:

- **Form:** "Aura Platform LLC" — the legal name in full. The
  `LLC` suffix is doctrine-mandated (Section 4.3): the company
  is the *institution*, and dropping the suffix would slide it
  toward a product-brand reading.
- **Typeface:** Inter, semibold, display weight, slight negative
  letter-spacing.
- **Case:** sentence case, with `LLC` in capitals as the legal
  abbreviation it is.
- **Tracking:** restrained — no expanded letter-spacing,
  no all-caps treatment.
- **Colour:** ink on paper (or paper on ink) — never the
  institutional teal, which is an *accent*, not a brand colour
  for the wordmark itself.

This wordmark already appears in canonical form in the
institutional band at the foot of every public surface
(company / founder / Bajwa Writes), and in the company site's
masthead. It needs no design work; it needs preservation.

## 3. If a symbol exists — why, where, how does it avoid
competing with products?

The narrow case for a silent functional device:

### Why

A favicon slot exists at `company.auraplatform.org` and may
exist at future technical-square contexts (a macOS bookmark
icon, an OS-level web-app shortcut, an embedded preview thumbnail
in tools that don't render Open Graph). These slots are too small
for the wordmark to remain legible. The platform draws *something*
in them — either what we provide, or a generic placeholder.

The choice is between:
- A generic, identity-less placeholder (the browser default
  globe glyph or a coloured square with the first letter
  auto-filled).
- A small device that says "the institution exists here" without
  attempting to brand.

The second is better. The first reads as a misconfigured site.

### Where

The functional device is permitted **only** at:

- `favicon.ico`, `favicon-16x16.png`, `favicon-32x32.png` at
  `company.auraplatform.org`.
- `apple-touch-icon` and PWA manifest icons (192 × 192, 512 × 512,
  maskable variants) if the company site is ever installed as a
  PWA — currently it is not.
- An OS bookmark / preview-tile thumbnail context that
  technically requires a square image.

It is **not** permitted at:

- The site masthead. The wordmark alone, every time.
- Slide footers in the investor deck.
- The institutional band at the foot of public surfaces. The
  band is wordmark + ecosystem nav, not a logo lockup.
- Beside the wordmark in any text-set context.
- On any product surface. Product mastheads carry the product's
  identity, not the company's.
- On any social-preview / OG card. OG cards use the wordmark
  and canonical content visuals (CG-01, etc.).

### How does it avoid competing?

Five rules, all must hold:

1. **Derived from existing canonical material, not newly designed.**
   The device must be a small, monochrome fragment of the
   already-canonised substrate-fingerprint diagram (CG-01) — the
   same diagram that already appears as a flagship visual on the
   company site. It is *not* a new mark; it is a quotation from
   the company's own architectural canon.
2. **Monochrome only.** No colour. No teal accent. The favicon
   slot does not earn an accent.
3. **Abstract — no letterforms, no monogram.** No `A`, no `APL`,
   no `AP` lockup. The doctrine in Section 4.2 explicitly
   excludes "another product"-style branding; a letter-form
   monogram is exactly that.
4. **Used only where the platform requires a square slot.** If
   the slot can be left blank without breaking the platform
   (e.g. a desktop browser will render no favicon and the page
   still works), leaving it blank is the *better* choice. The
   device fills slots that cannot be left blank.
5. **Never grows past favicon scale.** The largest the functional
   device appears is at the maskable 512 × 512 PWA scale. It
   never appears at 1024 × 1024 (which would be an app-store
   icon, and the company is not an application). It never
   appears as a hero, never as a masthead, never as a footer
   ornament.

Together these rules keep the device subordinate to the wordmark
in every brand-voice context and ensure it cannot compete with
Aura's typed-gate iconography or Orchestrate's circular-arrows
iconography on any shared surface — because it does not appear on
shared surfaces.

## 4. Browser tab strategy

The favicon at `company.auraplatform.org` is, per Section 3 above,
a small monochrome fragment of the CG-01 substrate-fingerprint
diagram — or, equivalently and acceptably, a *typographic*
favicon using a single character (an Inter semibold capital `A`,
treated as the publication's first letter rather than as a
monogram).

Both candidates honour the wordmark-only doctrine because
neither becomes a brand glyph at any larger scale.

**Preferred:** abstract device from CG-01. Reading as
architecture, not as initials.
**Acceptable alternative:** single Inter semibold `A`, monochrome.
Reading as a typographic character, not as the `A` monogram the
parent doctrine withdrew.
**Rejected:** the `APL` lockup, "AP" with a stylistic flourish, a
wordmark compressed into a square (illegible at favicon scale).

Each candidate requires its own short asset sub-brief before
production. This brief approves the *strategy*; production is a
separate step.

## 5. Company website strategy

**`company.auraplatform.org` masthead is the wordmark, alone.**

The page itself already implements this. The masthead at the
top is "Aura Platform LLC" set in Inter semibold; the nav links
to Ecosystem / Architecture / Literary / Investor / Founder /
Contact follow on the right. No icon to the left of the
wordmark; no decoration.

The body of the company site carries the canonical flagship
visuals (CG-01 fingerprint, CG-03 custody model, CG-04
ecosystem topology, AU-01 accountability, OR-01 readiness, OR-02
enforcement, BW-01 literary continuity). These are *diagrams*,
not logos — they are the architectural canon the company exists
to maintain, rendered at full literary scale in dedicated
sections. They are not the company's brand mark; they are the
company's content.

This brief codifies that distinction. The diagrams may
appear at any size demanded by the content. The favicon device
(if commissioned) may appear only at favicon scale.

## 6. Investor-deck strategy

A deck PDF or on-screen deck carries:

- **Cover slide.** "Aura Platform LLC" wordmark large, in display
  weight, centred or left-anchored. A one-line institutional
  caption ("Continuity infrastructure for institutions") in
  italic serif below. No favicon device on the cover.
- **Content slides.** Wordmark at the foot of each slide in
  restraint — JetBrains Mono small caps, optical centre or
  bottom-left, at ≤ 11 pt. Page number opposite.
- **Architectural slides.** When a slide is built around CG-01
  or any flagship diagram, the diagram occupies the slide centre;
  the wordmark footer is unchanged. The diagram is the *content*
  of the slide, not a corner-mounted logo.
- **Closing slide.** Wordmark + contact information + the
  institutional band naming the five members of the ecosystem.

No slide carries the favicon device. No slide carries a
gradient, a sparkle, a "next-gen" badge, an "AI-powered"
flourish. The deck is set in the same restraint as the website.

## 7. PDF strategy

Identical to the deck strategy. Long-form company PDFs
(white papers, governance memos, investor narratives) carry:

- **Title page** — wordmark, document title, date, revision.
- **Running footer** — wordmark in mono small caps + page
  number.
- **Body** — single-column reading at literary line length,
  Inter for prose; Source Serif 4 reserved for quoted literary
  material where it appears.
- **Diagrams** — flagship visuals (CG-01 etc.) at content
  scale, never as corner-mounted logos.
- **Colophon** — closing page naming the revision, the
  publication date, and the canonical URN of the document if
  the document is content-addressed.

No company PDF carries a logo glyph at the brand-voice level.
The wordmark is the brand voice.

## 8. Relationship to Aura

**Aura is louder; the company is quieter.**

On any surface where both identities appear (a product page,
a press release announcing the product, a slide presenting
Aura's architecture), Aura's wordmark and mark are the visual
foreground. The company's wordmark appears in the institutional
band at the foot — *with* Aura's name in the band, listed
alongside the other ecosystem members.

The company never *frames* Aura. Aura is not "an Aura Platform
LLC product" in the marketing sense; it is "Aura, an
infrastructure built by Aura Platform LLC" — a structural
attribution, set in the institutional band, not as a corporate
caption above the product mark.

The company's substrate-fingerprint diagram (CG-01) explicitly
shows Aura and Orchestrate sharing one architectural pattern.
This diagram is the company's deepest visual claim; it appears
*as content* on the company site and decks, never as decoration
on a product surface.

## 9. Relationship to Orchestrate

**Identical pattern to Aura (Section 8).**

Orchestrate carries its own mark (the circular-arrows iconography
realigned to the canonical brand source in the recent
store-asset commit). On shared surfaces, Orchestrate's mark
comes forward; the company sits at the institutional foot.

The same CG-01 diagram on the company site that explains Aura's
governance pattern also explains Orchestrate's — they instantiate
the same five-slot architecture. This is the company's
architectural argument for existing as the umbrella over the two
products; the argument lives in the diagram, not in a logo.

## 10. Relationship to Bajwa Writes

**Bajwa Writes is in the literary register; the company sits
at the institutional foot.**

The Bajwa Writes site (`bajwawrites.com`) ships in the warm
paper register (Section 5.5). The company wordmark appears at
the foot of every Bajwa Writes public surface as part of the
institutional band, set in the institutional dark register
even when the body above is in literary paper. This intentional
register-shift at the foot signals that Bajwa Writes is published
*by* Aura Platform LLC, without superimposing the company's
register on the literary work.

The company does not appear on Bajwa Writes book covers, on
publication-page hero compositions, or on essay imprint pages
except as a quiet caption ("Published by Bajwa Writes, an
imprint of Aura Platform LLC") on the colophon.

## 11. Social-preview strategy

A company OG card composes from:

- **Background:** dark institutional register
  (`#0E1326`/`#151B33`).
- **Primary visual:** the substrate-fingerprint diagram (CG-01)
  or another canonical flagship visual, rendered as the
  centerpiece. The diagram is the company's most-recognisable
  asset; using it on the OG card reinforces "this is the
  institution behind the three substrates".
- **Type block:** wordmark "Aura Platform LLC" at display weight,
  one-line caption below ("Continuity infrastructure for
  institutions"), set in restraint.
- **No favicon device on the OG card.** The favicon device, if
  it exists, lives at favicon scale and nowhere else.
- **No marketing voice.** No "Series A", no "valuation", no
  "trusted by", no badges.

The existing company site references
`assets/flagship/cg-01-fingerprint-dark.svg` as its OG image.
This brief approves that direction; a future asset task may
produce a PNG sibling if the existing SVG is rejected by any
legacy social-preview crawler (per the audit note on legacy
Facebook fetchers).

## 12. Prohibited / rejected / permanently excluded

### Prohibited (no exception)

- **The `A` monogram** in any treatment — bold, geometric,
  blackletter, stylised, neon, gradient. The withdrawn drift.
- **The `APL` acronym** as a brand mark. The company is named
  in full ("Aura Platform LLC") in every brand-voice context.
- **The abbreviated form "Aura Platform"** without the `LLC`
  suffix as a logotype. The legal suffix is doctrinal.
- **A product-shaped logo** — anything that reads as the
  company's own application icon. The company is not an
  application.
- **A holding-company logo wall** — multi-square grids of the
  five ecosystem members' marks. The institutional band, set
  as a horizontal text strip, is the canonical form.
- **A "founded by" or "by [founder]" lockup** placing the
  founder's wordmark inside the company's identity. The founder
  and the company are two surfaces; lockups merge them
  inappropriately.
- **Any colour outside dark institutional register + teal
  accent** for company-identity contexts. The literary paper
  register belongs to Bajwa Writes.

### Rejected (declined in this brief; could be reconsidered in
a future explicit amendment)

- **An animated logo** at any tier (favicon, masthead, OG card).
  Restraint excludes motion at the identity level (Section 5.2).
- **A wordmark with an underline or rule frame.** The wordmark
  carries itself.
- **A two-line wordmark stacked** ("Aura Platform" over "LLC").
  Single-line is canonical.
- **A favicon device in colour.** Monochrome only at favicon
  scale.
- **A favicon device with motion or interactivity.** Static.

### Permanently excluded motifs

- The `A` monogram (any form).
- The `APL` acronym.
- Holding-company logo grids.
- Founder-and-company lockups.
- Gradient or neon treatments.
- Any visual louder than what Aura or Orchestrate carry on a
  shared surface. The company is the substrate beneath, not the
  banner above.

## Recommendation

| Decision | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **Brand-level symbol** | **Optional — narrow, technical, silent.** The wordmark "Aura Platform LLC" is the brand at every brand-voice surface. A small monochrome favicon device (derived from the existing CG-01 fingerprint, or a typographic single `A`) may exist *only* at technical-square slots where a wordmark cannot render. |
| **Favicon** | An abstract monochrome fragment of CG-01 is preferred; a single Inter semibold `A` is acceptable. Subject to its own pre-asset sub-brief if commissioned. |
| **Masthead** | Wordmark alone. No glyph to the left. |
| **Institutional band** | The horizontal text strip listing Company / Aura / Orchestrate / Bajwa Writes / Founder is the company's primary visual presence and stays as wordmark text — no logo lockups. |
| **OG card** | Background diagram from canonical flagship visuals (CG-01 etc.) + wordmark + one-line caption. No favicon device on OG. |
| **Deck / PDF** | Wordmark on cover, running mono footer, diagrams at content scale, no corner-mounted logo. |

The company's identity is, by doctrine and by this brief, the
quietest of the three meta surfaces (Bajwa Writes / Founder /
Company). The substrate is not loud. The substrate is what the
loud things stand on.
